# Mass Spectrometry Course Materials

## Chapter 1: Fundamentals of Mass Spectrometry

### 1.1 What is Mass Spectrometry?
Mass spectrometry (MS) is an analytical technique that measures the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of ions. It's used to identify and quantify molecules in simple and complex mixtures.

### 1.2 Basic Components
1. Ion Source - converts molecules into ions
2. Mass Analyzer - separates ions by m/z ratio
3. Detector - records the abundance of each ion

### 1.3 Key Concepts
- Resolution (R): R = m/Δm
- Sensitivity: Detection limit
- Mass accuracy: ppm error

## Chapter 2: Instrument Operation

### 2.1 Sample Preparation
- Dissolve sample in appropriate solvent
- Filter to remove particles
- Typical concentration: 1-10 μg/mL

### 2.2 Startup Procedure
1. Check vacuum system (< 10^-5 Torr)
2. Warm up ion source (30 minutes)
3. Calibrate mass axis
4. Run blank sample

### 2.3 Common Issues
- Low signal: Check sample concentration
- Peak broadening: Calibration needed
- No signal: Check vacuum, ion source

## Chapter 3: Data Analysis

### 3.1 Interpreting Spectra
- Base peak: Most abundant ion (100%)
- Molecular ion: [M]+
- Fragmentation patterns reveal structure

### 3.2 Quantification
- Use internal standards
- Calibration curves (linear regression)
- LOD and LOQ determination

## FAQ

Q: How to improve resolution?
A: Use higher resolution analyzer, optimize ion source parameters

Q: What causes baseline noise?
A: Electronic noise, chemical background, poor vacuum

Q: How often to calibrate?
A: Daily for routine work, before each run for high accuracy

## Safety Notes
- Always wear PPE
- Handle solvents in fume hood
- Check gas cylinders regularly
- Emergency shutdown: Red button on console
